Research

My research interests span Data Visualization, Computer-Supported Cooperative Work (CSCW), Human-Computer Interaction (HCI), Rendering, and Modeling and Simulation. I have several ongoing projects:

  • Systematic Review of Collaborative Data Visualization
  • Collaboration and CSCW Theory
  • Provenance and Awareness of Bias in AI Models
  • Adaptive Interfaces to Support References
  • Designing Periphery Plots
  • Supporting Orchestration for Data Visualization Classrooms

Systematic Review of Collaborative Data Visualization

Visualization techniques have changed significantly since the topic of Collaborative Data Visualization was defined over a decade ago. I am leading a large-scale systematic review of collaborative data visualization papers published across nine venues exploring five main themes: Project Design and Artifacts, Visualization Setup, Collaboration Setup, Population and Sample, and Scientific Rigor.

Collaboration and CSCW Theory

Collaborators can be viewed as multiple agents exchanging information within a context. I am developing a theory around reframing CSCW systems as computer-mediated interventions to overcome constratints on the exchange of information.

Provenance and Awareness of AI Bias

Provenance has proven useful as a technique for transferring knowledge between collaborators across asynchronous handoffs. I am exploring whether similar techniques can be used to assist asynchronous teams in uncovering bias in AI models assisting with decision-making tasks.

Adaptive Interfaces to Support References

CSCW systems provide significant opportunities to assist with referential communication between collaborators. I am examining how an adaptive interface can help adjust the context of users collaboratively analyzing datasets. By bringing divergent contexts into alignment, the system can assist collaborators in understanding each other's references.

Designing Periphery Plots

Periphery Plots summarize data around the main view of a data visualization to provide awareness to the analyst of the surrounding data context. I am leading a project to design novel visualization techniques to expand the utiliy of periphery plots to different kinds of data beyond temporal data.

Supporting Orchestration for Data Visualization Classrooms

Orchestrating classroom activities is a challenge in data visualization course, as existing learning management systems rarely support developing data visualizations. I am leading a project, supported in part by an NSF grant, to develop a tool for course instructors to orchestrate and provide personalized interventions in design-thinking focused activities in the classroom.
